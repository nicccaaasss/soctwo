What is Parliament?

The place where a country decides.

Parliament is where elected representatives gather to discuss the country's problems, propose solutions and turn ideas into law. It exists so that decisions affecting millions of people are made openly, through debate, rather than by one person alone.

Representation

Every constituency elects a member to speak on its behalf.

Debate

Ideas are argued openly before any decision is made.

Accountability

The government must answer to Parliament for its actions.

Law-making

Bills are examined, amended and voted on before becoming law.

Lok Sabha

The House of the People, elected directly by citizens.

Rajya Sabha

The Council of States, representing India's states and union territories.

Government & Opposition

The ruling party governs; the opposition questions and checks it.

The Speaker

Presides over the house and keeps debate fair and orderly.

How does Parliament work?

Two houses, one shared purpose.

Members of Parliament bring the concerns of their constituencies into the house. Together, the government and opposition debate, question and vote — turning discussion into direction for the country.

What is a bank?

A place that keeps money moving.

A bank holds people's money safely, and puts it to work — lending to businesses, funding homes, and paying interest to savers. It exists to connect people who have money with people who need it.

Safety

Deposits are held securely rather than kept in cash at home.

For individuals

Savings accounts, loans and payments for everyday life.

For businesses

Working capital and credit that lets companies grow.

Interest

The bank pays savers and charges borrowers to make this possible.

Opening an account

Loans

Borrowing against the future.

A loan lets someone use money now and pay it back later, with interest. The amount borrowed is the principal; the extra paid on top is interest. Eligibility depends on the borrower's ability to repay.

Collateral

What is collateral?

Collateral is something valuable a borrower offers as a guarantee. If the loan isn't repaid, the bank can claim it — which is why loans backed by collateral are usually easier to get.

Backed by an asset

Secured loan

The borrower pledges an asset — like property or gold — against the loan. Lower risk for the bank usually means a lower interest rate.

No asset required

Unsecured loan

Approved based on trust and the borrower's ability to repay alone. Higher risk for the bank usually means a higher interest rate.

Investment

When money moves, economies grow.

Deposits don't just sit still — banks invest and lend them out, which funds new businesses and jobs. In return, investments can earn a return, but every return carries some risk.
